How we calculate your number

No black box. This is the actual model, so you can judge it yourself.

  • Housing is looked up by region and comfort tier from reported rental ranges — Central Valley value towns run $500–$1,200/mo, Escazú/Santa Ana $800–$2,200/mo, value coastal areas $550–$1,400/mo, and Tamarindo/Nosara-tier coast $1,000–$3,000/mo.
  • Food & household starts around $350/mo for one adult, plus roughly $220 per additional adult and $150 per child, based on Costa Rican supermarket and market pricing.
  • CAJA (public health insurance) is estimated at 9% of your stated income, with a realistic floor around $70/mo — this matches the commonly reported 7–11% range residents actually pay.
  • Vehicle costs (insurance, fuel, maintenance, marchamo) are estimated at roughly $250/mo per vehicle.
  • Discretionary spending (dining out, entertainment) scales up in premium coastal areas, where the whole cost structure runs higher.

What this does not include: private school tuition, one-time moving/import costs, immigration legal fees, or property purchase costs. Those are real and often larger than the monthly number above — ask us if you want help sizing them.

Frequently asked questions

Can I retire in Costa Rica on $1,500 a month?
In a value Central Valley town like Atenas or Grecia, as a single modest-lifestyle retiree with no vehicle, $1,500 is tight but realistic for day-to-day living. It gets noticeably harder with a vehicle, a couple, or any coastal premium location — run the numbers above for your actual situation rather than relying on a headline figure.
What's the cheapest place to live in Costa Rica as an expat?
Central Valley towns outside the premium Escazú/Santa Ana corridor — places like Atenas, Grecia, San Ramón, Turrialba, or Heredia's outer cantons — consistently report the lowest all-in cost of living while staying close to the international airport and San José's hospitals.
Do I need private health insurance in Costa Rica?
Legal residents are required to enroll in CAJA, the public system, as part of maintaining residency status. Many expats add a private supplemental policy for shorter wait times and English-speaking private hospitals — that typically adds $100–$150/mo per adult.
Does the Pensionado visa require a Costa Rican pension?
No — it requires a lifetime pension of at least $1,000/mo from any legitimate source (Social Security, a foreign government pension, a qualifying private pension). A married couple can qualify together on a single $1,000/mo pension.
Can the Remote Worker visa lead to permanent residency?
No. It's a temporary stay permit — one year, renewable once, for a two-year maximum — and does not by itself convert into permanent residency. If long-term residency is the goal, Rentista or Pensionado is the actual track.
